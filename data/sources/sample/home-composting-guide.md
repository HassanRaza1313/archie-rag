# Home Composting Guide

## Why compost

Composting turns kitchen scraps and garden waste into a dark, crumbly soil conditioner. It keeps organic
material out of landfill, where it would otherwise break down without oxygen and release methane. Finished
compost improves soil structure, helps sandy soil hold water and helps clay soil drain.

## Greens and browns

A healthy pile balances nitrogen-rich "greens" with carbon-rich "browns". Greens include vegetable peelings,
coffee grounds, tea leaves and fresh grass clippings. Browns include dry leaves, shredded cardboard, straw and
wood chips. A good starting ratio is roughly three parts browns to one part greens by volume.

Too many greens make the pile wet, dense and smelly. Too many browns make it dry and slow. If the pile smells
of ammonia, add browns; if nothing seems to happen for weeks, add greens and a little water.

| Material | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vegetable peelings | Green | Chop large pieces to speed decomposition |
| Coffee grounds | Green | Paper filters can go in too |
| Dry leaves | Brown | Shred them first if you can |
| Cardboard | Brown | Remove tape and glossy coatings |
| Meat and dairy | Avoid | Attract pests and smell as they rot |

## Moisture and air

The pile should feel like a wrung-out sponge: damp but not dripping. Microbes need oxygen, so turn the pile
with a fork every one to two weeks. Turning also moves cooler outer material into the hot centre.

## Temperature

An active hot pile reaches 55 to 65 degrees Celsius in its centre. Holding that temperature for several days
kills most weed seeds and pathogens. A pile needs a volume of about one cubic metre to heat up properly;
smaller piles still compost, only more slowly.

## When is it ready

Compost is ready when it is dark brown, smells earthy and the original materials are no longer recognisable.
In a well-managed hot pile this takes two to three months; a cold, unturned pile can take a year. Let finished
compost cure for two weeks before using it around young seedlings.
