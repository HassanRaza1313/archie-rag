# Bicycle Maintenance Basics

## Before every ride

Squeeze both brake levers: they should feel firm and stop well before touching the handlebar. Lift each wheel
and spin it to check that it runs true and does not rub the brake pads. Check that the quick-release levers or
axle nuts are tight.

## Tyre pressure

Correct pressure prevents pinch flats and makes the bike roll efficiently. The allowed range is printed on the
tyre sidewall. As a rough guide, road tyres run at 80 to 100 psi, hybrid tyres at 50 to 70 psi and mountain
bike tyres at 25 to 35 psi. Heavier riders should stay towards the top of the range. Check pressure weekly,
because tyres lose air slowly even without a puncture.

## Chain care

A clean, lubricated chain shifts smoothly and lasts longer. Wipe the chain with a rag after wet rides. Every
few hundred kilometres, degrease it, let it dry, then apply one drop of chain lubricant to each roller and wipe
off the excess. Lubricant left on the outside of the chain only attracts grit.

A chain stretches as it wears. Measure it with a chain checker tool: replace the chain at 0.5 percent wear on
11- and 12-speed drivetrains, or at 0.75 percent on drivetrains with fewer gears. Replacing the chain in time
protects the far more expensive cassette and chainrings.

## Fixing a flat tyre

Remove the wheel, deflate the tube fully and lever one side of the tyre off the rim. Pull out the tube and run
your fingers carefully around the inside of the tyre to find the thorn or glass that caused the puncture.
Slightly inflate the new tube so it holds its shape, fit it, and roll the tyre bead back on with your thumbs
rather than levers to avoid pinching the tube.

## Brake pads

Rim brake pads have wear grooves; replace them when the grooves are gone. Disc brake pads should be replaced
when the friction material is thinner than about 0.5 millimetres. Squealing disc brakes are often
contaminated with oil and need new pads and a cleaned rotor.
